The Null Model Every Population Fails

Hardy-Weinberg says allele frequencies sit still. That is not a prediction about nature, it is a statement of what happens when nothing is happening — and its real use is telling you which assumption broke.

Pull the selection slider down to zero. The zigzag collapses to a single dot, sitting on the olive curve, and it stays there however many generations you ask for. That is the whole of the Hardy-Weinberg principle: with random mating and nothing else going on, allele frequencies do not move, and the genotype proportions are whatever the allele frequencies imply. It is not a law about populations. It is a description of what an absence looks like.

Reading the triangle

Every population with two alleles is three numbers that add to one, so it is one point in a triangle. The bottom-left corner is a population of nothing but aa, the bottom-right nothing but AA, and the apex one in which every individual is a carrier. Height above the base is the share of heterozygotes. The useful accident of drawing it this way is that horizontal position works out to be exactly the frequency of A — homozygotes contribute all of their alleles and heterozygotes half, which is the same arithmetic the triangle's geometry already does.

newborns at frequency p: p² AA, 2pq Aa, q² aa

That curve is the parabola those three numbers trace as p runs from 0 to 1, and one round of random mating puts any population onto it. Try it in your head: a population of 60 per cent AA and 40 per cent aa, with no carriers at all, has the same allele frequency as one with 30 per cent AA, 60 per cent carriers and 10 per cent aa. After a single generation both are the same population, 0.36 / 0.48 / 0.16, sitting on the curve. Mating erases the arrangement of the parents and keeps only the count.

What the deviation means

Now put selection back. Each generation the blue dot is the newborns, on the curve where mating left them, and the rust dot is who survives to breed. Removing three fifths of the aa individuals lifts the survivors above the curve, not below it: the lost aa homozygotes are replaced proportionally by everyone else, and carriers gain most because there are more of them. The first generation of newborns is 45.5 per cent carriers and its survivors are 61.0 per cent, which is the tall vertical jump at the left of the run. The next generation of newborns lands back on the curve, but further to the right.

How that rightward walk peters out is the real lesson. Thirty generations of culling three fifths of every aa individual take a from 0.65 down to 0.056; the next ninety take it only to 0.014, and the zigzags shrink to nothing as they go. Set selection to 1 — every aa individual dies before reproducing, the harshest selection possible — and the model has a closed form: one over the frequency of a rises by exactly one per generation. Getting a from one in ten down to one in a hundred takes 90 generations. Getting it from one in a hundred to one in a thousand takes 900. At a frequency of one per cent there are 198 carriers for every affected individual, and selection cannot see a single one of them.

Where it settles

Selection alone drives a recessive towards zero and never arrives. Add mutation and it stops somewhere: new copies of a appear at rate mu per generation, selection removes them at a rate that scales with q², and the two come level when mu equals s q². So the resting frequency is the square root of mu over s — the readout names it, and the self-test finds the model's own fixed point by bisection and confirms it agrees to one part in a hundred million. A square root is a weak lever: a mutation rate a thousand times lower buys only a thirty-onefold reduction in the allele.

What to try

Set selection to zero and mutation to its largest setting. The population slides towards the left-hand corner without ever leaving the curve — mutation changes the frequency but never the proportions, so this is a departure that Hardy-Weinberg proportions cannot detect at all. Now push selection back up to 1 with that mutation rate still on. The two pull in opposite directions and the run stops dead at p = 0.68, which is the square root of 0.1 taken off one, exactly where the readout says the balance should be.

20260920 + 3), weighting each page by `16 - batch`, so batch/000 pages were
sixteen times as likely to be drawn as batch/015 pages. The ten pages drawn by
AUDIT/001 and AUDIT/002 were excluded from the pool so the sample says
something new. Drawn:

## Notes per page

### bloom-filter-false-positives — 5.0

The most distinctive page in the sample and the strongest overall. The drawing
runs full width above the masthead and captions itself, the prose sets in two
columns below, and the graphite-and-amber palette with square corners and
monospace furniture looks like nothing else in the atlas. The page opens on a
probe that is already a false positive, at a configuration that is correctly
tuned rather than broken, so the subject is on screen in the first frame.
Thirteen assertions, several of them exact identities (doubling m squares the
rate at the optimal k; the optimal k leaves half the bits lit). The legend
names a teal that is absent at the opening step only because no probe has yet
come back correctly absent — checked against the source, not a defect.

### tragedy-of-the-commons — 4.8

Three stacked charts, seven controls and three buttons, and prose that gives
the algebra rather than gesturing at it: the overshoot is 2n/(n+1) and the
profit kept is 4n/(n+1)², and both are asserted. Thirteen assertions reaching
the equilibrium three independent ways — a best-reply fixed point, herders
feeling their way to it iteratively, and an exhaustive sweep.

One point off for the top chart. Total profit is a downward parabola that goes
negative once the herd passes break-even, and the chart floors it at the axis,
so it reads as flat at zero. The page's argument is that the common ends up
carrying nearly twice what it should; drawing the damage as zero rather than as
a dive understates precisely the thing being argued. Where the two markers
coincide the page stacks both labels rather than hiding one, which is the right
call and the opposite of the defect batch/015 had to fix twice.

### big-o-crossover — 4.6

The overhead slider is the whole argument and the page knows it. Thirteen
assertions, including the exact worst case of a top-down merge sort checked
against every permutation of up to seven elements, and counting by hand against
counting by running the sort.

One point off clarity: the prose says that on nearly ordered input "insertion
sort's bar collapses while merge sort's does not move", and the chart beside it
shows merge sort going 363 → 258 between shuffled and nearly ordered, a
twenty-nine per cent fall the reader can see. The claim the page wants is the
one it makes correctly elsewhere — merge sort is nearly unchanged between the
two *extremes*, 240 backwards and 240 sorted. The sentence should say that.
One point off visual for the dead white beside the figure, where the control
rail runs about 250px longer than the figure column.

### central-limit-theorem — 4.4

The prose is among the best in the collection: the two facts that come for free
at every n before any limit theorem, the skew/√n argument as the real content
of convergence, and the Cauchy case where the theorem fails outright. The lower
panel is an exact convolution rather than a simulation, which is the right
choice and is what lets the readouts be checked against closed forms.

Nine assertions, the fewest of any page in the atlas — the newest pages carry
thirteen or fourteen. They are all genuine, and the coin case checked against
binomial coefficients over 2ⁿ is a real second derivation. What is missing is
the pattern the atlas adopted from batch/003 onward: the failure case discussed
at length in the prose (infinite variance, the mean of n draws distributed
exactly as one draw) is never asserted. A page that explains where a theorem
breaks should check that it breaks. Not a repair — the page is sound, and the
gap is a ceiling on its score rather than a defect in it.

### special-relativity-time-dilation — 4.2, and V scores 2

Twelve strong assertions, including the spacetime interval surviving every
boost and a forward-then-back boost returning the original event, and prose
that derives γ from one right-angled triangle and then spends it on the muon.

The stage canvas at 1280px is about ten pixels too short for what it draws.
The last line painted is `moving clock: 24.00 ticks` and the bottom edge cuts
it through the middle; only the top half of the glyphs survives. `your clock:
48 ticks` above it renders in full. That is the page's headline comparison with
one of its two numbers unreadable. Re-rendered live from the current file to
confirm. At 390px both lines have room, which is why the batch/013 legibility
sweep missed it — that sweep was looking for what breaks as a canvas narrows.

Filed as `queue/141-repair-time-dilation-tick-readout.md`.

## Observed drift

**Visual distinctiveness remains the weakest axis, in all three audits**:
3.20, then 4.60, now 3.80. It is not falling — AUDIT/002 drew four pages that
had just been rebuilt under repair task 082, which is why that audit's V was
high — but it is consistently the axis that gives up the point. The recurring
shapes of it are two:

1. **Dead whitespace beside the figure** when the control rail is taller than
   the drawing. Two of the five sampled pages have it, and batch/015 had to fix
   the same thing on a page it was building that week. A drawing whose height is
   a constant will eventually sit beside a rail that is not.
2. **A drawn label that does not fit the canvas it is drawn in.** This is the
   third audit in a row to find one, and it is the only score of 2 in this
   sample. The verifier cannot see it: the text is inside a canvas, so
   `innerText` is clean and nothing throws.

**The new diagnosis, and it is worth carrying forward:** task 082 swept every
pre-batch/004 page for legibility and closed in batch/013, and it still missed
this one, because every check in that sweep asked what happens as the canvas
gets *narrower*. This defect appears as the canvas gets *wider* — the drawing
grows to fill the width, the layout runs longer than the height constant
allows, and the last thing drawn falls off the bottom. **A canvas whose height
is a constant while its width tracks its container is only ever checked at one
end.** Pages from batch/013 onward derive height from width, which is why the
defect stops there.

**Assertion rigor is rising with batch age, not falling**: the two oldest pages
in the sample carry 9 and 12 assertions, the three newer ones carry 13 each,
and the newest pages in the atlas carry 13 and 14. The practices the loop
adopted along the way — deriving the headline quantity a second independent
way, asserting the counterexample as well as the corrected claim — are visible
in the sample as a gradient. No correction needed; the trend is the right one.

**The running average has not fallen for three consecutive audits** (4.28,
4.80, 4.56 running), so no diagnosis goes to the top of `STATE.md` on that
count. The wide-canvas lesson above goes in as a standing correction on its own
merits.

## Notes per page

**greenhouse-energy-balance** — The strongest page in the sample. Three sliders
and four presets, arrow widths proportional to flux, and a diagram that makes
the downward arrow — the entire greenhouse effect — a thing you can point at.
The prose earns its fifth mark with *What the single layer leaves out*, which
names convection, wavelength dependence, latitude and ice–albedo feedback as
things the model cannot do; that honesty is what the atlas is for. Twelve
assertions, including each level balancing separately, ε=0 reproducing the bare
rock exactly and ε=1 giving exactly 2^(1/4). Marks off interaction only because
the four readout cards wrap badly at 1280 (*top-of-atmosphere imbalance* breaks
across three lines) and the figcaption floats far to the right of the diagram it
describes.

**shannon-channel-capacity** — The black terminal-styled chart with the shaded
impossible region is the most distinctive frame in the sample, and the
guess-then-reveal loop with a running score is a genuinely different reading
experience. Capacity derived twice — as 1 − H(p) and as a maximisation of mutual
information over input distributions — is exactly the practice batch/003
recommended. Marks off for only two parameters, the minimum the contract allows,
and for axis labels that vanish at 390px.

**damped-harmonic-oscillator** — Clean three-regime structure, and the closed
form agreeing with RK4 to twelve digits is a strong check. Visual distinctiveness
is the weak axis: the frame is the template default and the phase-portrait axis
labels ("velocity", "displacement") are set so small they are unreadable in the
1280 capture, never mind on a phone. Nine assertions is on the low side by
current standards.

**floating-point-precision** — Excellent writing and the most rigorous assertion
set in the sample: exhaustive over k = −24..60, a decode-and-rebuild round trip,
Kahan against naive summation. But the stage fails. At 1280 the tick labels,
the "one gap = 2.220e-16" annotation and the 2^−10 … 2^−70 axis are all near
the limit of legibility, and at 390 the canvas collapses into an unreadable grey
smear — the page's primary visual channel is simply gone on a phone. There is
also an empty white box below the bit-field legend. **Scores 2 on visual
distinctiveness; repair task filed.**

**numerical-integration-error** — The derivation of E_strip = −(h³/12)f″(ξ) and
E_pair = −(h⁵/90)f⁗(ξ) is good, and the page is honest about √x destroying the
convergence order and about hitting the floating-point floor. But the log-axis
labels on the lower error plot are borderline at 1280, the stage column leaves a
large empty area below the figure because the right rail is taller, and eight
assertions is the fewest in the collection.

## Observed drift

There is no quality drift — the two batch/003 pages score higher than the two
batch/000 pages on every axis where they differ. What the sample shows instead is
a **legacy defect concentrated in the oldest pages**, in exactly the area
batch/004 learned to fix:

1. **Undersized canvas typography.** Three of the five (floating-point-precision,
   damped-harmonic-oscillator, numerical-integration-error) draw labels at sizes
   chosen for a 1000px-wide canvas and never reconsidered. All three are from
   batch/000–001.
2. **No narrow-screen relayout.** None of the five has a `layout()` that changes
   canvas dimensions or pane arrangement below ~620px. batch/004 established that
   scaling a wide canvas into a phone viewport produces an illegible strip and
   that enlarging fonts alone does not fix it. Every page merged before batch/004
   predates that lesson.
3. **Unbalanced two-column stages.** Where the control rail is taller than the
   canvas, the stage column ends in dead whitespace. Visible on
   floating-point-precision and numerical-integration-error.

Interaction quality (3.60) and visual distinctiveness (3.20) are the two weak
axes and they share a single root cause: the oldest pages treat the canvas as a
fixed-size illustration rather than as a responsive surface. Clarity, assertion
rigor and correctness are all at or near ceiling and need no intervention.

## Recommendation for the next batches

The narrow-screen relayout and a minimum canvas type scale should be treated as
part of building a page, not as polish. Neither is a contract change and neither
needs a proposal — the contract already requires the page to be responsive to
360px, and these pages are only nominally meeting it.

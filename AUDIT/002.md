## Notes per page

**auction-formats-revenue** — The best page the atlas has produced. It runs
both auction formats on the *same* drawn valuations, so the claim it is making
(that two visibly different bid distributions collect the same money) is
demonstrated rather than asserted, and the two running averages converging on
one dashed theory line is the entire revenue equivalence theorem in a picture.
The assertions go past the page's own prose: the first-price equilibrium bid
is checked against the integral equation it was derived from, not just against
its closed form, and the revenue-maximising reserve is shown to be one half
for every number of bidders, which is the non-obvious half of Myerson. The
dark stage panel and the single full-width column make it look like nothing
else in the collection.

**monty-hall** — Real doors that are really clicked, with the reader's own
play tracked separately from the simulation, and the ignorant-host setting that
turns the puzzle from a trick into a lesson about conditioning. The generalised
odds (N-1)/(N(N-2)) are asserted for N = 3 to 12 rather than the usual
three-door special case. Marked down one point on assertion rigor only: nine
assertions is the smallest count in the sample, and the Monte Carlo check uses
a three-standard-error band where later pages compare against a measured
sampling floor. Neither is wrong; both are looser than the current bar.

**newton-raphson-basins** — The exact two-cycle of z^3 - 2z + 2 trapping the
origin and the threefold rotational symmetry of the basins are exactly the
right things to assert about a fractal, because they are properties no
rendering bug could accidentally satisfy. Interaction marked 4: panning and
zooming a basin picture is inherently a less pointed action than moving a
parameter and watching a number move, and the page cannot help that.

**pid-controller** — Tuning three gains to match a target curve is the most
satisfying interaction in the sample, and the assertions are unusually strong
for a simulation page: the steady-state error 1/(1+Kp), the second-order
overshoot formula, and a Routh-Hurwitz stability boundary that the simulation
is then made to actually cross. Visual distinctiveness 4 rather than 5 because
the two-stacked-panes arrangement is the collection's most common shape.

**equal-temperament** — Correct and well-assertioned; the Pythagorean comma and
the published cents table are the right reference points. Marked 4 on both
interaction and visual distinctiveness: the page is a comparison of two fixed
alternatives with a note selector, which gives the reader less to do than the
parameter pages, and the two-wave-panels-over-a-bar-chart layout is close to
several other pages.

## Drift since AUDIT/001

The two axes AUDIT/001 flagged have both moved a long way:

| axis | AUDIT/001 | AUDIT/002 | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| explanatory clarity | 5.00 | 5.00 | — |
| interaction quality | 3.60 | 4.60 | +1.00 |
| visual distinctiveness | 3.20 | 4.60 | +1.40 |
| assertion rigor | 4.60 | 4.80 | +0.20 |
| correctness | 5.00 | 5.00 | — |

Visual distinctiveness is the headline. AUDIT/001 found every pre-batch/004
page treating its canvas as a fixed-size illustration, and filed repair task
082 for it; eleven pages have now been rebuilt on the container-tracking
pattern, and three of the five pages in this sample are among them. The axis
has moved because the defect was fixed, not because the sample was kind.

Two things worth watching rather than acting on:

1. **Assertion counts are drifting upward** — 9 to 12 in this sample against
   13 on every page merged in batch/010. That is not a problem in itself, but
   it means the oldest pages will keep looking thin by comparison. The useful
   distinction is not the count: it is whether the page derives its headline
   quantity a second independent way. `auction-formats-revenue` does;
   `monty-hall`, which predates that practice, does not.
2. **The two-stacked-panes canvas is becoming the house shape.** Both pages
   marked down on visual distinctiveness in this sample were marked down for
   it. The pages that stand out — `auction-formats-revenue` with its dark
   stage and single column, `monty-hall` with its centred column and clickable
   doors — differ in *page* layout, not just in canvas arrangement. Varying
   the canvas inside the same two-column template is not enough.

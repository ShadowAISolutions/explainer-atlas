# 062 — le-chatelier-equilibrium-shift

**Domain:** chemistry
**Suggested interaction pattern:** `perturb-and-observe`

## Concept

Disturbing an equilibrium shifts it in the direction that partly undoes the disturbance, and the equilibrium constant does not move unless the temperature does.

## Why it benefits from interaction

Adding reactant, squeezing the volume and heating, each as a separate nudge, separates the three cases that students routinely merge.

## What the reader should be able to do afterward

Predict the direction of a shift from a disturbance and say which disturbances change K and which do not.

## Assertions the selftest must make

- the reaction quotient equals K at every computed equilibrium, to 1e-10, over 80 seeded systems
- adding reactant leaves K unchanged to 1e-14 while shifting the extent of reaction towards products
- halving the volume shifts the equilibrium towards the side with fewer moles of gas, in every tested case with unequal mole counts
- with equal mole counts on both sides a volume change shifts nothing, to 1e-12
- the van 't Hoff equation reproduces K at a second temperature to 1e-9 given the enthalpy change
